12. What the audit found, in ten lines:
    1. Every "Nd on market" on the site was `today minus listedAt`; the `daysOnMarket` column is null on all 2,930 rows, so the list date itself was the leak.
    2. The listing page rendered the day count in five places, one of them the meta description, so the SERP snippet carried it.
    3. The listing page serialised the whole Prisma row to the client: `listedAt`, `priorPrice`, `priceChangedAt`, `lastPriceChangeAt` in the HTML of every page.
    4. 604 sold and 1,044 expired listings answered 200 `index, follow`, sold ones labelled "SOLD", expired ones "FOR SALE" with "Status: expired" in the facts.
    5. 229 leased units answered as "FOR RENT" with "Status: rented"; `Offer.availability: InStock` on all of the above.
    6. `/listings?status=sold` listed the 604 sold homes with "Sold for $X" over the asking price, `soldPrice` being null.
    7. `/listings?status=rent` filtered on transaction type only, so leased units sat in the grid and every rental card was badged "Leased".
    8. `/rentals`, the homepage, the school and mosque pages and the three ad surfaces plus their slider all printed a day count per listing.
    9. The mega menu on every page struck through the prior price and printed "down $40,000" per card.
    10. Sold price itself was never rendered (the column is null); the k-gated street, hub and market-watch days-on-market figures are aggregates and were listed, not counted.
